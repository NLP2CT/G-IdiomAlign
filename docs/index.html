01 / THE IDEA

Meaning travels.
Words take different routes.

A shared semantic reference for culturally grounded expressions.

How do we know that two idioms in different languages mean the same thing?

Literal word matching is unreliable for non-compositional expressions. G-IdiomAlign uses English glosses from Wiktionary as a shared semantic pivot, connecting idioms through descriptions of their meaning.

The benchmark combines a precision-first alignment pipeline with two complementary evaluation protocols. Together, they reveal where models recover figurative meaning, where surface cues mislead them, and how explicit semantic support changes generation.

Read the abstract +

Idioms are difficult to transfer across languages due to their non-compositionality and weak surface-form grounding, making literal mappings unreliable. We present G-IdiomAlign, a gloss-pivoted benchmark where each idiom is anchored by an English gloss from Wiktionary. We further construct a high-confidence reference alignment set for reproducible evaluation. G-IdiomAlign supports two protocols: (1) a controlled Multiple-Choice Idiom Equivalence with typed distractors for error attribution; and (2) a Gloss-Contrastive Generation contrasting No-gloss and With-gloss inputs to isolate the effect of an explicit semantic pivot. Across diverse LLMs, a bias to literal translation is a dominant failure mode, especially when the target is a low-resource language. Glosses consistently improve Gloss-Contrastive Generation under an embedding-based semantic proxy, but performance remains modest, indicating substantial headroom in the open output space. Subsequent analysis on Qwen3-8B further suggests that cross-condition differences are concentrated more in attention heads than in layers, while better With-gloss generations coincide with stronger gloss anchoring.

HOW THE REFERENCE SET IS BUILT

A precision-first construction pipeline.

  1. 01

    Extract the meaning

    Collect Wiktionary idioms and core English glosses. Retain single-sense entries.

  2. 02

    Retrieve candidates

    Find top-10 candidates in a shared gloss-embedding space.

  3. 03

    Require agreement

    Keep rank-1 mutual nearest neighbors under both language directions.

  4. 04

    Filter within each pair

    Apply a distribution-aware cutoff calibrated to each language pair.

Benchmark scope and interpretation +

A precision-first reference. Single-sense filtering and one-to-one matching improve interpretability while limiting polysemous and many-to-many correspondences. English glosses may compress cultural and pragmatic nuance.

Alignment quality is not uniform. On sampled non-Zh–En pairs, LLM voting gives 68.5% strict and 92.3% lenient equivalence, averaged over language pairs. Human evaluation on 200 Zh–En pairs gives 65.5% strict and 89.5% lenient equivalence. Lenient evaluation includes partially equivalent pairs; these figures are not model task accuracy.

Read generation scores comparatively. Fixed embedding thresholds are proxies. Correct alternatives can differ from the canonical reference, and high similarity alone does not establish idiomatic correctness. The paper also reports human calibration and surface-form metrics.
